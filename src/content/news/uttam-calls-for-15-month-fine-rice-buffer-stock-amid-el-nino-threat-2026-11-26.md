With the farming community complaining about delays in procurement, Civil Supplies Minister N Uttam Kumar Reddy directed officials to expedite fine paddy procurement and build sufficient fine rice buffer stocks to meet Telangana’s Public Distribution System (PDS) and institutional requirements for at least 15 months.

The directive comes amid uncertainty over emerging El Nino conditions, which have cast a shadow over agricultural prospects in several States.
At a review meeting with officials, Reddy said the State must prepare well in advance to insulate its food security system from any impact if agricultural production is affected by adverse climatic conditions.

Reddy said Telangana currently has sufficient fine rice stocks to meet its requirements for around 14 months and that it would be prudent to build a substantially higher buffer in view of the uncertainty surrounding the coming agricultural seasons.

He directed officials not to scale down paddy procurement operations and emphasised the need to maximise procurement of fine varieties to ensure uninterrupted supplies under the State’s flagship fine rice distribution scheme.

Officials were also directed to widely publicise at procurement centres the eligibility of the seven notified fine paddy varieties for the Rs 500-per-quintal bonus to avoid confusion among farmers.

In a major initiative aimed at strengthening the Civil Supplies Corporation’s infrastructure, Reddy emphasised the need to create 10 lakh metric tonnes of modern silo-based storage capacity across Telangana.

He said that while paddy production and procurement in Telangana have increased significantly over the years, storage infrastructure has not expanded commensurately, forcing the Corporation to depend heavily on private rice millers during every procurement season.

Reddy directed officials to identify the State’s top 10 paddy procurement clusters, taking into account procurement volumes during both Kharif and Rabi seasons, and prioritise the establishment of storage facilities in these areas.

The Minister directed officials to explore internal funding and institutional borrowing, with particular emphasis on securing loans at the lowest possible interest rates and with the longest repayment tenure.

The proposals would be placed before the Cabinet Subcommittee and subsequently the State Cabinet for approval.
